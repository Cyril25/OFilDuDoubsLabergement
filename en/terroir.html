Food and wine of Franche-Comté

Jura wines, cheeses and smoked meats from the Haut-Doubs

The Haut-Doubs is also something to discover at the table. Its wines come from the vineyards of Franche-Comté, its cheeses from village dairies, its cured meats from the smoke of the great farmhouse chimneys. This page introduces these specialities, their history and the best pairings. A selection of wines and craft drinks is available on a self-service basis in the apartment, and the fir syrup is our gift to you on arrival. The cheeses and cured meats are just a short walk away, in the village shops.

Jura wines

In the west of Franche-Comté, on the hillsides that border the plain, lies a small wine region with highly distinctive wines, grouped under the Jura appellation. Five grape varieties are grown there: Chardonnay and Pinot Noir, well known elsewhere, and three that are emblematic of the region: Savagnin, Poulsard and Trousseau.

Our selection brings together seven of them, to accompany a whole meal, from aperitif to dessert. The Crémant is kept in the fridge, the other wines in a small glass-fronted wine cabinet at 12 °C. The cuvées change with each delivery, but the spirit stays the same: wines from winegrowers and wine cooperatives, chosen to go with the local cooking.

Topped up or under a veil: the key to the region's white wines

In the barrel, wine slowly evaporates. The winemaker then has two choices. If the barrel is regularly topped up, the wine stays protected from the air: this is called ouillage, and it gives fresh, floral, mineral whites. If the level is left to drop, a thin veil of yeast forms on the surface, protects the wine and slowly transforms it: it develops notes of walnut, spice and dried fruit, the famous “goût de jaune”. This is the secret of vin jaune, which spends more than six years under its veil, and of the wines known as “typé”.

Crémant du Jura

Sparkling · white

Its secret
The bubbles of the Franche-Comté vineyards, made as in Champagne by a second fermentation in the bottle: the traditional method. The appellation has existed since 1995.
On the palate
Fine bubbles, a fresh and lively palate, notes of white fruit and citrus.
At the table
As an aperitif, with cubes of young Comté or Comté gougères.

Serve at 6–8 °C

Chardonnay

Dry white · topped up (ouillé)

Its secret
The world's most famous white grape takes on a particular accent here, on marl and limestone soils. Ours is ouillé: protected from the air throughout its ageing, it keeps all its freshness.
On the palate
Dry, round and mineral, with notes of white flowers.
At the table
Trout, Comté fondue, Comté aged a few months.

Serve at 10–12 °C

Savagnin

Dry white · typé, aged under a veil

Its secret
The region's great white grape, the one used for vin jaune. Ours is “typé”: it has aged under a veil, without topping up, and it is this veil of yeast that gives it its very particular taste.
On the palate
Dry and broad, with notes of fresh walnut, gentle spice and dried fruit. A long finish that surprises with the first sip.
At the table
The natural partner of aged Comté, baked Mont d'Or, fondue and creamy dishes such as chicken with vin jaune.

Serve at 12–14 °C

Jura rosé

Dry rosé

Its secret
A rosé from the region in a classic style, chosen for its lightness and fruit, to be drunk young.
On the palate
Dry, light and fruity, with notes of small red berries.
At the table
Cured meats, mixed salads, Montbéliard sausage.

Serve at 8–10 °C

Poulsard

Light red · also called Ploussard

Its secret
The red that people mistake for a rosé. Its grapes have thin skins with few pigments, which gives the wine a very pale, almost translucent colour, often compared to coral. The village of Pupillin, which has made it its speciality, calls itself the capital of Ploussard.
On the palate
Light, with very soft tannins and notes of small red berries.
At the table
Morteau sausage, cured meats, poultry. Serve it slightly chilled.

Serve at 12–14 °C

Trousseau

Red

Its secret
Deeper in colour and more structured than Poulsard, it remains an elegant red. The village of Montigny-lès-Arsures, near Arbois, calls itself its capital.
On the palate
Ripe red and black fruit, a hint of pepper, a round palate.
At the table
Morteau sausage with lentils, grilled meat, game.

Serve at 14–16 °C

Macvin du Jura

White fortified wine (vin de liqueur) · 16 to 22% vol.

Its secret
The fermentation of the grape juice is stopped, just as it begins, by adding grape marc brandy from the same estate; the blend is then aged for at least a year in oak barrels. Macvin thus keeps the sugar and fruit of the grape. An old tradition that became an appellation in 1991.
On the palate
Sweet and lively at the same time: grape, dried fruit, honey, with the brandy well integrated.
At the table
Well chilled, as an aperitif or with dessert: melon, foie gras, walnut desserts.

Serve at 8–10 °C

Maison Rième, Morteau

Since 1921, Maison Rième has been making lemonades and syrups in Morteau, in the heart of the Haut-Doubs. Founded by Marcel Rième and his brother-in-law Adrien Bouhéret, it has always remained in the family, now in its fourth generation.

La Mortuacienne

Craft lemonade · in the fridge

Its secret
Its name pays tribute to the Mortuaciens, the people of Morteau. Created by Marcel Rième, it became the emblem of the house, and its recipe is still a closely guarded secret.
On the palate
Sparkling and thirst-quenching.
At the table
For the whole family, after a hike, or with a Cancoillotte.

Fir syrup

Our gift on arrival

Its secret
The fir is the tree of the Haut-Doubs: it covers the forests around the apartment and scents the smoke of the tuyés. Maison Rième has turned it into a syrup, which we offer you for your stay.
How to enjoy it
Diluted with cold still or sparkling water.

Cheeses of the Haut-Doubs

Here, cheese is a matter of raw milk, meadows and cooperatives. Farmers deliver their milk to the village fruitière, which turns it into cheese on site. Four cheeses sum up this know-how.

Did you know?

In Franche-Comté, a fruitière is a cooperative. Dairy farmers pool their milk there to make Comté; winegrowers do the same with their grapes in the fruitières vinicoles. Same word, same idea: producing together, in the village.

Comté

AOP · cooked pressed cheese

Its secret
It takes about 400 litres of raw milk from Montbéliarde or Simmental cows to make a 40 kg wheel. Ripening lasts at least four months, often much longer (eight months on average, sometimes two years): every wheel has its own character.
On the palate
Young, it is mild and milky, with notes of butter and hazelnut. With age it becomes fruitier and more powerful, and develops small crystals.
At the table
Chardonnay with a young Comté, Savagnin with an aged one: the region's most famous pairing.

Mont d'Or

AOP · soft cheese, bound in spruce

Its secret
The winter cheese. It is only made from 15 August to 15 March, and only sold from 10 September to 10 May. As soon as it is made, it is bound with a strip of spruce bark, then ripened in its spruce-wood box, which gives it its woody aroma.
On the palate
Creamy enough to eat with a spoon, with notes of undergrowth and resin.
At the table
Baked in the oven as a “boîte chaude”, with a dash of white wine and potatoes. And a glass of Savagnin.

Morbier

AOP · uncooked pressed cheese

Its secret
You can recognise it by its black line. According to tradition, farmers protected the curd from a first milking with a thin layer of soot, until they could complete the cheese with milk from the next milking. Today the line is made of tasteless vegetable charcoal, and it has remained as a signature.
On the palate
A supple, melting texture with a mild, milky taste that grows stronger with age.
At the table
Chardonnay or Poulsard. Melted as raclette, it works wonders.

Cancoillotte

IGP · melted cheese

Its secret
The most surprising of all. The milk is skimmed to make a small cheese, the metton, which is left to ripen before being melted. The result is a runny cheese cream, awarded IGP status in 2022.
On the palate
Plain, with garlic or with wine, cold or warm, on bread or hot potatoes.
At the table
A fresh Chardonnay, or a Mortuacienne.

Cured meats smoked in the tuyé

The tuyé

In the old farmhouses of the Haut-Doubs, a huge pyramid-shaped wooden chimney dominates the house: this is the tuyé. Hams and sausages are hung there above a slow fire of softwood and sawdust, which dries them and gives them their flavour. This tradition is listed in France's inventory of intangible cultural heritage.

Morteau sausage

IGP · smoked over softwood

Its secret
The queen of the region's cured meats, slowly smoked over softwood and sawdust. You can recognise it by its little wooden peg. And one detail links the whole terroir: the pigs are partly fed on whey from the local cheese dairies.
On the palate
Firm, smoky, very aromatic. It is cooked gently in simmering water, without pricking it, so that it keeps its juices.
At the table
Lentils, potatoes, Cancoillotte… and a Poulsard or a Trousseau.

Montbéliard sausage

IGP · smoked, seasoned with cumin

Its secret
Its cousin from the Montbéliard area: slightly curved, seasoned with cumin and pepper, and also smoked over softwood.
On the palate
Finer than the Morteau, juicy, with the warm touch of cumin.
At the table
In a potato salad or with hot Cancoillotte, and a rosé or a Poulsard.

Smoked ham and brési

Smoked and dried

Their secret
Haut-Doubs ham goes through salt, the smoke of the tuyé, then long months of drying. Brési is a speciality of salted, dried and smoked beef, cut into very thin slices.
On the palate
The ham is tender and deeply smoky. Brési is lean, concentrated and dark red.
At the table
On a plate, with a Poulsard or a Trousseau. Brési as an aperitif, with a Crémant.
